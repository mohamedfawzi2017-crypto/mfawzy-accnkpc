Subject: Submission of Original Research Manuscript

I am pleased to submit our original research manuscript entitled:
"Asymmetric Cost-Channel Monetary Transmission and Policy-Rate Thresholds: Evidence from Egypt"
for consideration for publication as a regular research article in The Journal of Economic Asymmetries.

Following our previous successful contribution published in your esteemed journal, this study addresses a central, unresolved puzzle in open-economy monetary economics: the empirical "Price Puzzle" and its asymmetric structural inversion across monetary regimes.

Key Contributions of this Manuscript:
1. Theoretical Micro-foundations: We derive a micro-founded Hybrid Asymmetric Cost-Channel New Keynesian Phillips Curve (ACC-NKPC) integrating working-capital credit frictions with a continuous smooth aggregation function, overcoming standard discontinuity issues in discrete menu-cost models.
2. Endogenous Threshold Inversion: Utilizing a comprehensive monthly dataset (2011M01–2026M04, T=184) from the Central Bank of Egypt, two-step optimal GMM and Hansen threshold estimation reveal an endogenous switching boundary at i* = 20.00%. Below 20.0%, monetary tightening transmits positively to inflation (+1.88 pp per 100 bps hike, p < 0.001) due to acute working-capital cost dominance. Above 20.0%, the cost channel is neutralized, re-establishing classical disinflationary demand transmission.
3. Asymmetric Pass-Through & Welfare Loss: We document profound exchange-rate pass-through asymmetry (+6.20 pp cumulative inflation surge per 10% depreciation, versus near-zero pass-through for appreciations). The calibrated welfare deadweight loss amounts to 0.84% of annual household consumption expenditure (EGP 1,428 per household/year, aggregating to EGP 38.5–40.0 Billion annually nationwide).
4. Machine Learning & Replication Integrity: Tree-based ensembles (Gradient Boosting, Random Forest) provide non-parametric out-of-sample corroboration, recovering an inflection near 20.0%. The complete replication suite and master dataset are made publicly available on GitHub.
